★ YOUR STUDENTS ARE THE SHARKS ★

It's 1792–1837. Every student gets $1,000. Five promoters pitch real ventures of the Transportation Revolution, and students decide on the spot how much to invest. Four ventures really succeeded. One was a real failure, sold with a con artist's tricks. Can your class spot it before the money's gone?

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

HOW IT WORKS
① Meet the promoter and see what they're selling
② Hear a voiced 90-second pitch with captions and illustrated exhibits
③ Grill the promoter: the class picks questions, and the answers hide real clues
④ Invest from $0 to everything you have left (no take-backs!)
⑤ Where Are They Now? Newspapers reveal the real outcome and why it mattered

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

WHAT'S INCLUDED
✔ Interactive website: 5 voiced pitches, 45 illustrated exhibits, 30 Q&A cards, and the Where Are They Now reveal
✔ Student reading: "The Transportation Revolution" (2 pages)
✔ Check for Understanding: 6 multiple choice + 6 true/false
✔ Investor Tracker student worksheet with simple whole-number math
✔ Build Your Thesis writing activity (Although → Claim → Because)
✔ Teacher tools: pitch order by period, answer calculator, leaderboard and debrief notes
✔ Catch-up mode for absent students

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

HISTORICAL CONCEPTS TAUGHT
• The Transportation Revolution and the rise of a market economy
• Turnpikes and toll roads (the Lancaster Turnpike)
• The factory system, water power and child labor (Slater's Mill → the Lowell mills)
• Steamboats, monopolies and Gibbons v. Ogden (1824)
• The Erie Canal, locks, and the growth of New York City
• Early railroads, internal improvements and the Panic of 1837
• Henry Clay's American System, and how North, West and South grew apart

SKILLS: judging claims against evidence • spotting hype and red flags • cause and effect • reading comprehension • thesis writing

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GRADES: 8–12 U.S. History
TIME: 2–3 class periods (reading, game, thesis)
TECH: a projector and a web browser. No logins, no downloads.

Voices and character art were created with AI tools. All history content was written and fact-checked for this lesson.
